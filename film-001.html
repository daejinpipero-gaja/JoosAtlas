02 Claims

What the film states, set against sources.

A film is not a source. Each statement below is placed beside a public document, or marked as resting on the film alone.

A hot-work spark is 0.3 to 3 mm across and hotter than 1,⁠600 °C.0:02 · 0:19

Matched to a source

The KOSHA technical guideline on fire prevention in welding and cutting gives both figures. It was read in its 2016 reprint; the current edition, KOSHA GUIDE A-G-14-2026, was not opened for this check.

Guideline as reprinted in 2016 ↗

With wind or a bounce off structure, a spark can travel more than 15 m.0:28

Film only

No public source for 15 m was found in this check. The same KOSHA guideline posts a fire watch where combustibles in quantity lie within 11 m of the work.

A spark can smoulder inside combustible material for minutes to hours.0:33

Matched in part

The KOSHA guideline states that a spark can start a fire through stored heat a considerable time after it lands. It gives no time range, so “minutes to hours” rests on the film.

Burning urethane foam releases hydrogen cyanide.1:17

Matched to a source

A 2016 review in Fire Science Reviews names carbon monoxide and hydrogen cyanide as the two major asphyxiants from burning polyurethane foam, with yields that depend on ventilation. An earlier literature review of rigid foams by NBS, now NIST, reports the same pair.

McKenna & Hull (2016), Fire Science Reviews 5:3 ↗

Paabo & Levin (1985), NBSIR 85-3224 ↗

Over five years there were 2,⁠732 construction-site fires, 1,⁠300 of them from sparks.1:32

Matched to a source

National Fire Agency statistics for 2020 to 2024, as reported on 18 February 2025: 2,⁠732 fires, of which 2,⁠049 were attributed to carelessness and, within those, 1,⁠300 to welding, cutting and grinding. The film’s “sparks” is that last category.

Newsis, 18 February 2025 ↗

Three rules: clear what can burn; post a fire watch and block the scatter; keep extinguishing and escape provisions.1:41

Matched in part

The Rules on Occupational Safety and Health Standards require, for fire-risk work, protection of nearby combustibles, extinguishers at hand and spark-scatter measures such as welding blankets (Article 241), and a designated fire watch (Article 241-2). Since 2 March 2026 welding blankets must be performance-certified. The escape-facility part of the third rule was not traced to an article in this check.

Ministry of Employment and Labor notice, 26 January 2026 ↗
